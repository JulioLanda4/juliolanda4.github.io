About

Julio Landa’s profile: doctoral research on sensor-based feedback for heliostat control, supervised by Dr. Camilo Arancibia at IER-UNAM.

I have been a PhD student in Renewable Energy at IER-UNAM since 2026 and am a mechatronics engineer. Under the supervision of Dr. Camilo Arancibia, I research closed-loop control of solar heliostats using computer vision and complementary sensing methods.

I develop sensor-based feedback methods for heliostat positioning, with primary emphasis on computer vision. I carry out experimental work with the heliostat, solar furnace and control system at IER-UNAM. I also serve as a teaching assistant for the Solar Thermal Energy course, particularly solar geometry.

I consider myself a dedicated, versatile, meticulous, and creative person. I enjoy working in a team and learning from other perspectives, although I also greatly value working on my own, focused and in silence. This combination has accompanied me in different projects and experiences, and has given me a balance between the collective and the personal.

Sports play an important role in my life: I play soccer and athletics, which have taught me discipline, perseverance, and the satisfaction of gradually improving myself. Drawing and photography are hobbies that allow me to relax and keep my creative side alive, with no other goal than to enjoy the process.

Traveling and learning about other cultures is another important part of my life. It helps me broaden my vision and adapt easily to different contexts, something that I also transfer to my personal and academic projects.

Right now, I am motivated to continue growing in research and science, with a special interest in solar thermal energy, automation, bioclimatic design, and data analysis. I seek to find a balance between the technical and the human: to create solutions that are born out of curiosity and rigor, but that bring practical value to everyday life.
